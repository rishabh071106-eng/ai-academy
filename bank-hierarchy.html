Job hierarchy levels across banks,
mapped to neutral bands

Titles don't compare 1:1 across firms — a “Vice President” can mean four different things at four different banks. This page maps each firm's ladder onto six neutral bands anchored on typical experience and scope, so you can compare positions without title bias. Covers the major investment banks plus custodian banks like State Street, BNY and Northern Trust.

Bands are defined by typical years of experience and job scope — not by any one firm's titles. Years are indicative for front-office paths; individual progressions vary.

State Street, in detail

State Street's full internal ladder has more rungs than a classic investment-bank ladder, and two of its titles trip people up: MD sits below SVP, and officer titles arrive much earlier than at the banks above.

Associate 1→ Associate 2→ Senior Associate→ Officerfirst exempt / officer grade→ Assistant VP→ Vice President→ Managing Director→ Senior VP→ Executive VPsenior leadership / C-suite pool
Rough market equivalence: a State Street AVP maps to a Band-2/3 role (associate to junior VP) at the investment banks above; a State Street VP maps to Band 3–4 (VP to junior Director); a State Street MD maps to Band 4 (Director/ED); and a State Street SVP is the closest equivalent of an investment-bank MD (Band 5). This isn't a knock on anyone — custodian and trust banks historically hand out legal “officer” titles (AVP/VP/SVP) tied to signing authority, so the same words simply sit at different points on the ladder.

How to compare titles without bias

  • Anchor on band, not title. When benchmarking a person or an offer, ask: years of relevant experience, team managed, clients/deals/P&L owned, and compensation range. Two “VPs” can be two bands apart.
  • “Vice President” is the most overloaded title in finance. It's Band 3 at most US investment banks, Band 4 at State Street and Northern Trust, and a legal officer designation at commercial banks where thousands of people hold it.
  • “Director” flips meaning by firm. At UBS it's a mid-level (Band 3); at BofA, Barclays, Deutsche and Citi it's senior (Band 4); at Goldman “Executive Director” is just the EMEA/Asia name for VP.
  • Custodian-bank titles are structurally richer. State Street, BNY and Northern Trust promote to officer titles earlier and add extra rungs; discount their AVP/VP/SVP titles by one to two bands when comparing with a bulge-bracket front-office role.
  • Some ladders are longer than others. Citi has the most rungs from entry to MD; Goldman has the fewest distinct titles (its VP band just lasts longer). More rungs ≠ slower careers — total years to the top band are broadly similar.
  • Region and division matter. The same firm can use different titles in EMEA vs. the US, and back-office/technology ladders often carry different (usually richer) titles than front office at the same band.
