02

The business you serve

A custodian keeps perfect records of who owns what, moves assets safely when told to, handles every chore that ownership creates, and shows clients all of it. You own the showing.

The machine, top to bottom

ClientsPension funds, asset managers, insurers, sovereign funds. Their users are operations analysts, portfolio managers, treasurers and executives.
Digital ExperienceClient portal, APIs and data delivery, alerts, documents and reports, dashboards, identity and entitlements. The windows into the machine. Yours.
OperationsThousands of people in settlements, corporate actions, reconciliations, fund accounting, client service. They own the truth your screens display.
Core systemsCustody and accounting books of record, many on mainframes with end-of-day batch cycles of six to ten hours. Your "real-time" sits on a periodic heartbeat.
Market networkSub-custodians in 100+ markets, national depositories (CSDs), SWIFT messaging, payment rails such as Fedwire and TARGET2.

Six things the machine does, each in three sentences

Custody and safekeeping

Assets are electronic records held in a chain: client, State Street, a local sub-custodian, the country's central securities depository. State Street never owns them. Perfect records are the entire product.

Number1 bp on $46T ≈ $4.6B revenueYour screenHoldings, by account, as of a date

Trade settlement (T+1)

Every trade is agreed, affirmed, then settled by a simultaneous swap of shares and cash (delivery versus payment). US markets settle one business day after trade since May 2024. When a side is not ready the trade fails and penalties and phone calls begin.

NumberAffirm by 21:00 ET on trade dateYour screenExceptions first, then status

Fund accounting and NAV

Every night, for thousands of funds, the value of one share is computed: assets minus liabilities divided by shares. People buy and sell at that number. A wrong NAV means recalculating and compensating real money.

NumberError threshold ≈ 0.5%Your screenIs tonight's NAV out, and why did it move

Corporate actions

Companies announce dividends, splits, tender offers and rights issues. Some just happen; some require the client to choose by a deadline. A missed voluntary election is custody's classic multi-million loss, and it fails in the last mile of human attention.

NumberCustodian deadline < market deadlineYour screenDeadline tracker with escalating alerts

SWIFT and cash

Banks instruct each other over SWIFT, a secure messaging network that moves messages, never money. Money moves through accounts banks hold at each other and national payment rails with hard cutoff times. The industry is migrating from MT messages to ISO 20022.

Number11,000+ member institutionsYour screenCash by currency, today and projected

Asset servicing chores

Owned assets generate income to collect, foreign tax to reclaim, votes to pass through, statements to produce. Each is small; multiplied by millions of holdings across 100 markets it is why custodians employ tens of thousands.

Number100+ markets via sub-custodiansYour screenDaily reports and document delivery

The pattern you will see all year: the boring 99% takes care of itself. Products earn their keep on the painful 1%. Great custody screens show the exceptions first.

Your domain: Digital Experience

You own the channel layer: every surface through which an institutional client sees, receives or instructs on their assets, and the standards that make those surfaces feel like one bank.

The six surfaces

SurfaceWhat clients do thereWhat breaks trust
Client portalLog in, see holdings, cash, trades, NAVs, corporate actions; instruct and elect.A number that differs from the report; a status that lags the phone call.
APIs and data deliveryPull the same data into their own systems: REST APIs, files over SFTP, increasingly live shares into their cloud warehouse.Schema changes without notice; a nightly file that arrives late.
Alerts and notificationsHear about fails, deadlines, NAV delays, cash shortfalls, in time to act.Spam, so people mute it. Or silence, so they miss a deadline.
Documents and reportingStatements, confirmations, regulatory reports, generated and retained for years.Anything deleted; anything unsearchable.
Dashboards and analyticsPortfolio views, exposure, activity, embedded BI.Three dashboards, three answers.
Identity and entitlementsSingle sign-on, who may see which account, delegated administration.One user seeing another client's account. Career-ending.

Your permanent stakeholders

  • Operations own the truth your screens display. Befriend the settlements, corporate actions and fund accounting leads first.
  • Technology and enterprise architecture build and run it. They decide what is possible this year.
  • Client coverage and service sell it and field complaints. Their ticket queue is your best research.
  • Risk, compliance and information security constrain it. Involve them early; late involvement is how releases die.
  • The Alpha platform team, whose front-to-back story you make tangible.

Three tensions you will inherit

  • Parity versus progress. Clients demand every feature of the old portal before adopting the new one. Agree a capacity split with your manager before the first escalation tests it.
  • One portal versus many. Custodians accrete portals through acquisitions and product silos. Expect a consolidation programme, and expect it to be older than your tenure.
  • Whose backlog wins. Custody, fund services, middle office and Alpha all want your team. Without an explicit prioritisation frame, the loudest business head wins.

Programmes you should expect to find in flight

  • Portal consolidation and modernisation, usually a strangler-fig migration: new pages behind a routing facade, legacy retired page by page.
  • API marketplace and developer experience: making data products self-serve for clients' engineers, with versioning and entitlements.
  • T+1 aftermath: the US moved in May 2024; the UK and EU are targeting 2027. Same-day affirmation, real-time status and alerting are still being hardened.

  • Cloud data delivery: replacing nightly SFTP files with governed live shares into clients' warehouses.
  • Generative AI for client service: copilots that answer "where is my report" from governed data. Model-risk sign-off is the gating factor, not the model.

Tech and data vocabulary

Twenty-four terms you will hear in the first week, each with the sentence you can safely say back.

TermMeaningSay it like this
Book of recordThe system whose numbers are legally the truth (custody core, accounting core)."Which book of record does this page read from?"
EOD batchThe overnight cycle that strikes balances and produces the day's files."What time does EOD complete, and what is the as-of stamp on the portal?"
As-of dateThe business date a number is true for, which is not the time you looked at it."Is this intraday or as of last night's close?"
Read storeA copy of data kept for fast page loads so the portal never queries the mainframe per click."How fresh is the read store behind holdings?"
Anti-corruption layerAn adapter that translates core-system codes into clean domain terms so core changes break one component, not twelve."Do we have an ACL in front of the core, or do its codes leak into the UI?"
Strangler figReplacing a legacy system by routing traffic to new components page by page until nothing is left."What percentage of page views still hit the legacy portal?"
Event backbone (Kafka)Systems publish facts ("trade settled") that others subscribe to, instead of being polled."Which events exist today, and who owns the schema?"
API productAn interface treated like a product: documented, versioned, entitled, measured on adoption."How many clients consume this API, and what is the deprecation policy?"
EntitlementsWho may see or do what, per account. Enforced in the data layer, not just the UI."Where is the entitlement filter applied, in the query or in the screen?"
SnowflakeCloud data warehouse with separate storage and compute; supports live secure data sharing."Which warehouse runs client-facing queries, and what is its auto-suspend?"
Data martThe governed, modelled layer a business domain reads from. Client-facing anything reads marts, never raw."Is this dashboard reading the mart or a private copy?"
Star schemaFact tables of measurements joined to dimension tables of context."What is the grain of this fact table?"
GrainWhat one row represents. The contract that decides what a table can answer."One row per account per security per date, or per lot?"
ELT and dbtLoad raw first, transform inside the warehouse with version-controlled SQL and tests."Are the transformations under version control with tests?"
Data lineageThe traceable path from book of record to a number on a screen."Can we trace this figure back to source for the auditor?"
CollibraData governance tool: glossary, catalogue, lineage, ownership, policy workflows."Is this field defined in Collibra, and who is the steward?"
Data quality dimensionsCompleteness, accuracy, timeliness, consistency, validity, uniqueness."Which DQ checks block the load, and which only warn?"
Freshness SLAA promise like "positions available by 06:00 on 99% of business days"."What is the published SLA, and how do clients see a breach?"
TableauBI tool for dashboards; can be embedded in a portal."Which extracts refresh overnight, and are any unused?"
SSO and IAMSingle sign-on and identity and access management: how a client user gets in and what they get."Do clients federate their own identity provider, or do we issue credentials?"
SFTP deliveryNightly files pushed to clients. Still the dominant channel at most custodians."How many files a night, and what is the failure rate?"
ISO 20022The XML-based message standard replacing SWIFT MT messages."Which of our flows are on ISO 20022 already?"
Model risk (SR 11-7)Bank rules for validating any model, including AI, before it influences decisions."Has this use case been through model-risk review?"
Change lead timeTime from merged code to production for the client-facing stack."What is our real change lead time, and what gate dominates it?"

You will never write production SQL. You will sit in reviews where a number is defended with a query, and the VP who can read it commands the room. This lab gives you a realistic custody dataset, thirteen queries that each power a portal screen, and three places to run them.

B. Route 1: run it in sixty seconds, no install

Python ships with SQLite, a full SQL engine in a file. macOS has Python; on Windows install it from python.org (tick "Add to PATH").

On Windows the command is py run_sqlite.py if python3 is not recognised.

E. Read the thirteen queries the way a VP reads them

Each query in queries.sql is labelled with the portal screen it powers. Three of them, with what to notice:

Q3. The portfolio pie: a client's value by asset class, across all its accounts

SELECT s.asset_class,
       ROUND(SUM(p.market_value_usd), 2) AS mv_usd,
       COUNT(DISTINCT p.account_key)     AS accounts_holding
FROM fact_positions p
JOIN dim_security s ON s.security_key = p.security_key
JOIN dim_account  a ON a.account_key  = p.account_key
WHERE a.client_key = 'C-MERIDIAN' AND p.position_date = '2026-09-11'
GROUP BY s.asset_class
ORDER BY mv_usd DESC;
asset_class | mv_usd | accounts_holding Government Bond | 487,042,465.08 | 1 Equity | 112,662,416.00 | 1 Corporate Bond | 76,701,520.00 | 1 Cash Equivalent | 936,746.88 | 2

Notice the two joins: the fact table only knows keys; the dimensions supply names and classes. Notice the date filter: without it you would sum five days of positions and quintuple the client's wealth. That mistake ships to production more often than anyone admits.

Q5. The exceptions screen: every trade that has not settled, with why

SELECT t.trade_id, a.account_name, s.security_name, t.side, t.quantity,
       t.trade_date, t.intended_settle_date, t.status, t.fail_reason, t.counterparty
FROM fact_trades t
JOIN dim_account  a ON a.account_key  = t.account_key
JOIN dim_security s ON s.security_key = t.security_key
WHERE t.status <> 'SETTLED'
ORDER BY CASE t.status WHEN 'FAILED' THEN 0 WHEN 'PENDING' THEN 1 ELSE 2 END,
         t.intended_settle_date;
trade_id | account_name | security_name | status | fail_reason T-90003 | Meridian Global Equity | SAP SE | FAILED | COUNTERPARTY_INSUFFICIENT_SECURITIES T-90006 | Meridian Global Bond | Bund 0.5% 2031 | FAILED | INSUFFICIENT_CASH_EUR T-90011 | Orient General Account | Toyota Motor | FAILED | SSI_MISMATCH T-90009 | Halcyon US Large Cap | Microsoft Corp | PENDING | ... (7 rows)

The ORDER BY is product design in SQL: failed first, then pending, then everything else. Three fail reasons, three different owners: the counterparty, the client's treasury, and a settlement-instruction mismatch that is probably the custodian's own reference data.

Q7. The corporate-action deadline tracker

SELECT ca.event_id, ca.event_type, c.client_name, s.security_name,
       ca.client_deadline, ca.market_deadline, ca.election_status, ca.est_value_usd
FROM fact_corporate_actions ca
JOIN dim_account  a ON a.account_key  = ca.account_key
JOIN dim_client   c ON c.client_key   = a.client_key
JOIN dim_security s ON s.security_key = ca.security_key
WHERE ca.election_required = TRUE AND ca.election_status IN ('PENDING', 'DEFAULTED')
ORDER BY ca.client_deadline;
event_id | event_type | client_name | client_deadline | election_status | est_value_usd CA-5005 | TENDER_OFFER | Halcyon Asset Management | 2026-09-10 | DEFAULTED | 2,700,000 CA-5003 | DIVIDEND_OPTION | Meridian Pension Trust | 2026-09-12 | PENDING | 360,000 CA-5002 | TENDER_OFFER | Meridian Pension Trust | 2026-09-15 | PENDING | 4,100,000 CA-5007 | RIGHTS_ISSUE | Meridian Pension Trust | 2026-09-18 | PENDING | 650,000

One event already defaulted with $2.7 million at stake. That row is the reason alerting is a platform, not a feature. The rest of the queries cover NAV sanity checks (Q8), running cash balances (Q9), day-over-day change (Q10), ranking within groups (Q11), a freshness SLA check (Q12) and an entitlement view that only ever shows one client's rows (Q13).

H. How to talk about data on Day 1

Five questions that make engineers and analysts respect you immediately. Every one of them maps to a query above.

  • Grain. "What does one row represent?" If a fact table is per lot and the screen sums per security, numbers double.
  • Join keys. "Joining on ticker or on security key?" Tickers get reused; ISINs and internal keys do not.
  • As-of and freshness. "What business date is this, when did it land, and what is the promise?" Q12 is that promise as a query.
  • Entitlements. "Where is the client filter applied?" If the answer is "in the front end", you have found your first risk item.
  • Cost. "Which warehouse runs this, and does it auto-suspend?" A zombie warehouse over a long weekend burns four figures for nothing.
