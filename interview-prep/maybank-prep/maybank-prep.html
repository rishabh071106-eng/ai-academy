4 · Core banking — heart of the bank JD

Core banking is the central system of record holding customer accounts and processing the fundamental transactions: opening accounts, deposits, booking loans/financing, posting debits/credits, calculating interest/profit, and maintaining the general ledger (GL). (“CORE” ≈ Centralised Online Real-time Environment.) Everything else — apps, ATMs, lending workflows — reads from and writes to the core.

ModuleOwns
Customer / CIFCustomer Information File — the single golden record (the “360 view”).
CASACurrent Account & Savings Account — deposits, balances, posting.
Loans / FinancingLoan accounts, schedules, interest/profit accrual, delinquency status.
General LedgerDouble-entry accounting — every transaction posts here.
Term depositsFixed deposits / term financing.
Payments & clearing hooksLinks to RENTAS, DuitNow, IBG, cheque clearing.
Islamic banking moduleShariah-compliant accounting — profit not interest (see §10).
PO talking point

“The core is the system of record; we keep it thin and stable and build innovation in a digital/microservices layer that talks to it through APIs and events — ship fast on the edges without risking the ledger.” That's the modernisation story MBB Labs exists to deliver.

Accuracy note

Most Malaysian domestic banks (Maybank included) have historically run core banking on Silverlake Axis's platform — but internal stacks evolve (cloud, microservices). In the room, speak to patterns and present vendor names as “industry-reported,” not confidential fact.

The end-to-end loan lifecycle (Malaysia / Maybank)

1 Application→ 2 Credit assessment→ 3 Approval & pricing→ 4 Offer & disbursement→ 5 Servicing→ 6 Delinquency→ 7 Rehabilitation→ 8 Recovery→ 9 Settlement
StageWhat happens
1 ApplicationApply on M2U/MAE, branch or agent; e-KYC vs MyKad; PDPA consent; pick conventional or Islamic.
2 Credit assessmentPull CCRIS + CTOS; verify income (payslip, EPF/KWSP, EA/BE tax form); compute DSR; BNM responsible-financing; auto-decision clean cases, refer complex.
3 Approval & pricingRisk-based SBR + spread (or Islamic profit rate with ceiling); approve/counter/decline; issue Letter of Offer.
4 Offer & disbursementAccept LO; sign facility agreement; create charge over property (National Land Code) or use Hire-Purchase Act 1967 for autos; stamp duty; MRTA/MRTT; disburse (progressive for under-construction).
5 ServicingInstalment via standing instruction/auto-debit/DuitNow; daily accrual; payment splits profit/interest + principal; statements; monthly CCRIS reporting.
6 DelinquencyTrack months in arrears (MIA); collections within fair-practice limits; impaired at ~90 DPD; drives GIL ratio.
7 RehabilitationR&R (reschedule/restructure: extend tenure, lower instalment, moratorium); refer hardship to AKPK.
8 RecoverySecured: foreclosure via court Order for Sale & auction (NLC) or vehicle repossession; unsecured: charge-off & recovery/sale; provisions under MFRS 9 ECL.
9 SettlementFull/early settlement or refinance; discharge of charge; close & report settled to CCRIS.

Islamic note: same flow — say “financing” not “loan,” “profit” not “interest.”

7 · Deposit management & asset/liability classes JD

Lending is the asset side; deposits are the liability side that funds it. The JD wants both, plus the balance-sheet view across “all asset and liability classes.”

Deposit (liability) products

ProductWhat it is
CASACurrent & Savings Accounts — low/no interest, can be withdrawn anytime. Cheap, sticky funding.
Fixed / Term DepositLocked for a tenure at a fixed rate; pricier funding. Islamic equivalent uses Tawarruq/Mudarabah.
NIDs / money-marketNegotiable instruments & wholesale funding.
MetricWhy it matters
CASA ratioCASA ÷ total deposits. Higher = cheaper funding = better margin. A headline bank KPI.
Cost of fundsWeighted rate the bank pays for deposits/borrowings — the floor under loan pricing.
NIM — net interest margin(Interest income − interest expense) ÷ assets. Core profitability of the asset+liability spread.

Asset–Liability Management (ALM)

ALM is the discipline of matching the asset book (loans, securities) against the liability book (deposits, borrowings) to manage three risks:

  • Interest-rate risk — assets and liabilities reprice at different speeds; if OPR moves, margin shifts.
  • Liquidity risk — can the bank meet withdrawals/obligations? Measured by LCR (Liquidity Coverage Ratio) & NSFR (Net Stable Funding Ratio) under Basel III.
  • Maturity mismatch — borrowing short (deposits) to lend long (30-yr mortgages) is the classic banking tension.
Balance-sheet framing

“A bank earns the spread between what assets yield and what liabilities cost, while managing rate, liquidity and credit risk. As a PO I design products on both sides — a financing product and the deposit product that funds it — with ALM and cost-of-funds in mind.” International markets add FX, bonds and money-market instruments across currencies.

8 · Rating systems & risk-based pricing JD

Credit risk rating system

A rating system converts a borrower/facility into a risk grade that drives approval, pricing, limits and capital. Two ratings combine:

RatingAnswersMaps to
Obligor / borrower ratingHow likely is this borrower to default?PD — probability of default
Facility ratingIf they default, how much do we lose?LGD — loss given default (driven by collateral)
  • Scorecards turn data into a score: application scorecards (at origination) and behavioural scorecards (ongoing, from account conduct).
  • Grades sit on a masterscale (e.g. 1–10 or AAA…D), each mapped to a PD band.
  • Under Basel's IRB (Internal Ratings-Based) approach, banks use their own PD/LGD/EAD to compute capital; the alternative is the Standardised approach.

Risk-based pricing

Price the loan to its risk. The rate is built up from components:

ComponentCovers
Cost of fundsWhat the deposits/borrowings funding the loan cost (links to §7).
Operating costOrigination & servicing overhead.
Expected lossEL = PD × LGD × EAD — the credit-risk premium from the rating.
Cost of capitalReturn on the regulatory capital held against the loan.
MarginTarget profit spread.

In Malaysia the customer rate is expressed as SBR + spread, where the spread embeds the above; Islamic uses a profit rate with a contractual Ceiling Profit Rate.

The chain to memorise

Rating → PD/LGD → Expected Loss → spread → price. A worse risk grade means a higher PD, a higher expected loss, and therefore a higher spread. This single chain connects the JD's “rating system” and “risk-based pricing” — say it out loud.

11 · PCI DSS, KYC/AML & data security JD

PCI DSS — protecting card data

PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard) is the mandatory standard for anyone who stores, processes or transmits cardholder data. It's not a law but it's enforced by the card networks; breaching it means fines and losing the ability to process cards.

Principle (of 12 requirements)In practice
Protect cardholder dataEncrypt the PAN (card number) at rest & in transit; mask when displayed.
Never store sensitive auth dataYou may never store the CVV, full magnetic stripe, or PIN after authorisation.
TokenisationReplace the PAN with a token so systems downstream never touch real card data — shrinks PCI scope.
Network segmentationIsolate the cardholder-data environment (CDE) from the rest of the network.
Access control & loggingLeast-privilege access, MFA, and audit trails on all access to card data.
Say this

“For any feature touching card data I design to minimise PCI scope — tokenise early, never persist CVV, segment the CDE — so we stay compliant and reduce breach surface. Compliance is a design input from story one, not a final gate.” That directly answers the JD's “PCI DSS compliance” line.

KYC / AML (links to the CAMS certification)

  • KYC — Know Your Customer: verify identity (CIP), understand the customer, risk-rate them.
  • AML — Anti-Money-Laundering: screen against sanctions/PEP lists, monitor transactions, file SARs/STRs (suspicious activity/transaction reports).
  • CDD / EDD — Customer / Enhanced Due Diligence for higher-risk customers.
  • PDPA (Malaysia) governs personal-data handling; design with consent & data-minimisation.

A product owner “bakes in” KYC/AML and data protection — capturing the right data points, consent, screening hooks and audit trails — rather than bolting them on later.

12 · System architecture diagrams — step by step

This is the part you were asked about last time. A system architecture diagram answers one question: “what are the parts, and how do they talk to each other?” Here's a method you can run live on a whiteboard.

The 8-step method

  1. Clarify scope & requirements first. Functional (what it does) + non-functional — scale (users/TPS), latency, availability, security, and compliance (BNM, PDPA, PCI, Shariah). Stating these out loud is half the score.
  2. Identify actors & external systems. Customers/staff on the left; outside systems (CCRIS, CTOS, PayNet, MyKad) on the right.
  3. List components by capability — “Origination service”, “Credit decisioning”, “Pricing”, “Servicing”. One box = one responsibility.
  4. Add data stores — each owning service gets a datastore (cylinder); mark the core & the warehouse.
  5. Group into layers — Channels → Edge/Security → Application → Integration → Core → Data. Layering shows flow direction.
  6. Draw & label every connection — direction + protocol + purpose: “REST/HTTPS – submit application”, “event (Kafka) – loan booked”, “SQL – read balance”, “SFTP – nightly CCRIS batch”. An unlabeled arrow is wasted.
  7. Mark trust boundaries & cross-cutting concerns — DMZ vs internal; where auth/MFA, the API gateway, WAF, logging & encryption sit.
  8. Validate & simplify — walk one journey through it, confirm every hop, then add a legend and keep shapes consistent.
Levels — the C4 model

Draw at zoom levels: Context (system + users/neighbours) → Container (apps, services, DBs) → Component (inside one service) → Code. Start at Context, zoom to Container, and say which level you're at.

14 · Worked example: Maybank lending architecture

A Container-level diagram for a digital home-financing journey. Note the layers, labelled connections, and regulatory externals. Practise narrating it top-to-bottom.

Narrate it in 30 seconds

“A customer applies on MAE or M2U. Traffic passes the WAF and API gateway, authenticated by IAM/MFA — that's the trust boundary. Origination orchestrates the journey, calling e-KYC (verifies MyKad via JPN), Credit decisioning (pulls CCRIS + CTOS, computes DSR, applies the rating), then Pricing (SBR + spread, or the Shariah profit engine). 16 · SQL basics for a banking PO JD

The JD asks for “basic SQL.” You won't write production SQL, but you'll read it, request data and validate numbers. Essentials on a tiny loan schema.

The mental model

Data lives in tables (rows = records, columns = fields), linked by keys: a primary key (PK) uniquely identifies a row; a foreign key (FK) points to another table's PK.

TableKey columns
customerscustomer_id (PK), name, mykad, income
loansloan_id (PK), customer_id (FK), product, outstanding, rate, status, months_in_arrears
paymentspayment_id (PK), loan_id (FK), paid_on, amount

The 6 clauses that do 90% of the work

-- SELECT what · FROM where · WHERE filter · GROUP aggregate · ORDER sort · LIMIT cut
SELECT product, COUNT(*) AS num_loans, SUM(outstanding) AS total_owed
FROM   loans
WHERE  status = 'active'
GROUP BY product
HAVING SUM(outstanding) > 1000000
ORDER BY total_owed DESC
LIMIT 10;

JOINs

-- Customer name next to each loan (INNER = only matching rows)
SELECT c.name, l.product, l.outstanding
FROM   customers c
JOIN   loans l ON l.customer_id = c.customer_id;
JoinReturns
INNER JOINOnly rows matching in both tables.
LEFT JOINAll left rows + matches (NULLs where none).
RIGHT / FULL JOINMirror of LEFT / both sides.

Banking queries you can talk through

-- Gross impaired loan (GIL) ratio by product
SELECT product,
       SUM(CASE WHEN months_in_arrears >= 3 THEN outstanding ELSE 0 END)
       / SUM(outstanding) AS gil_ratio
FROM   loans
GROUP BY product;
  • Aggregates: COUNT, SUM, AVG, MIN, MAX + GROUP BY / HAVING.
  • Filters: WHERE, DISTINCT, IN, BETWEEN, LIKE, IS NULL.
  • CTEs: WITH active AS (SELECT …) SELECT … FROM active.
  • Window functions: ROW_NUMBER() OVER(...), running totals (high-level awareness).
  • DDL vs DML: CREATE/ALTER/DROP define; INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE change; SELECT reads.
Interview-safe answer

“I'd GROUP BY product with SUM(outstanding), joining loans to customers on customer_id, filtering status='active'.” Showing you can shape a query and reason about joins is the goal — perfect syntax isn't required.

20 · Interview Q&A — drill these

Domain

Q

Walk me through the end-to-end loan lifecycle.

A

Application (e-KYC vs MyKad) → credit assessment (CCRIS + CTOS, verify income, DSR under BNM rules) → approval & pricing (rating → SBR + spread, or Islamic profit rate; Letter of Offer) → acceptance & disbursement (facility agreement, charge under NLC, stamp duty, MRTA) → servicing (instalments, accrual, CCRIS reporting) → delinquency (months in arrears, impaired ~90 DPD) → rehabilitation (R&R / AKPK) → recovery (foreclosure/repossession, MFRS 9 provisions) → settlement & discharge.

Q

How does a rating system connect to risk-based pricing?

A

The rating produces a PD (obligor) and, with collateral, an LGD (facility). Those give Expected Loss = PD×LGD×EAD, which becomes the credit-risk component of the spread. Worse grade → higher PD → higher EL → higher price. So: rating → PD/LGD → EL → spread → rate.

Q

Explain deposit management and why it matters to a lending PO.

A

Deposits (CASA, fixed deposits) are the liabilities that fund loans. The CASA ratio drives cost of funds, which is the floor under loan pricing and a key part of NIM. ALM matches the two sides for interest-rate and liquidity risk (LCR/NSFR). A lending product is only profitable relative to what its funding costs.

Q

What's the difference between conventional and Islamic financing?

A

Conventional charges interest (riba); Islamic avoids it by structuring a real transaction — Tawarruq for personal financing, Musharakah Mutanaqisah for home — earning profit with Shariah Committee approval. Same lifecycle, different contract and language.

Architecture & tech

Q

What is core banking and why not build everything in it?

A

Core banking is the system of record — CIF, CASA, loans, GL — where authoritative balances live. We change it rarely because ledger errors are catastrophic. Innovation goes in a digital/microservices layer that talks to the core via APIs and events, so we ship fast without risking the books.

Q

Draw the architecture for a digital financing journey. (See §14.)

A

Layer it: Channels (MAE/M2U) → Edge (WAF, API gateway, MFA = trust boundary) → Lending microservices (origination, e-KYC, decisioning, pricing) → Integration (ESB + Kafka) → Core (loans + GL) → Data. Externals: CCRIS, CTOS, MyKad, PayNet. Solid arrows = synchronous, dashed = async/batch. Then narrate one journey through it and name the protocol on each hop.

Q

Sync vs async — when do you use each?

A

Synchronous when the caller needs the answer now — balance check, payment auth, a credit decision the customer waits on. Async/event for background work — SMS, analytics, downstream updates. Events decouple services and protect the core from load.

Q

How do you keep a card feature PCI DSS-compliant?

A

Minimise scope: tokenise the PAN early, never store the CVV, encrypt card data at rest & in transit, segment the cardholder-data environment, and enforce least-privilege access with full logging. Compliance is a design input, not a final gate.

Product ownership

Q

You have 20 backlog items and one sprint — how do you prioritise?

A

Score value-vs-effort with RICE/WSJF, then overlay two banking non-negotiables: regulatory/Shariah must-haves and risk. Anything legally required or blocking go-live jumps the queue (MoSCoW “Must”). I sequence the rest by customer value and dependencies and socialise the trade-offs transparently.

Q

How do you write a good user story for a lending feature?

A

As a salaried applicant, I want an instant indicative financing amount, so that I know my eligibility. Then Given/When/Then acceptance criteria, plus explicit NFRs: PDPA consent, explainable decision for adverse-action, Shariah variant, audit trail. INVEST keeps it small and testable.

Q

How would you measure success of a new digital financing product?

A

A north-star (completed digital applications) + a balanced set: funnel conversion & drop-off, time-to-yes, digital adoption, approval/decline rates, NPS, cost-to-serve — and a guardrail the risk team owns: early-stage delinquency / NPL, so we're not “winning” by approving bad credit.

Q

How do you handle ambiguity and conflicting international stakeholders?

A

Align everyone on the outcome and the metric first; surface the disagreement explicitly; use data to choose; and de-risk by shipping a thin slice to learn rather than debating in the abstract. Across Bengaluru–KL I over-communicate in writing (Confluence) and keep one prioritised backlog (JIRA) as the single source of truth.

Q

A compliance constraint blocks the feature customers want. What do you do?

A

Compliance isn't optional, so I reframe: understand the exact rule (BNM/PDPA/PCI/Shariah) with risk, then find a compliant design that delivers most of the value — staged consent, an alternative contract, manual-now-automate-later. Document the trade-off and the path to the ideal state.

Behavioural (use STAR + T.I.G.E.R.)

Situation → Task → Action → Result, finishing on a quantified result. Have ready: a prioritisation trade-off (Excellence/Efficiency), a stakeholder conflict resolved (Relationship Building), mentoring a junior (Growth), a compliance-driven redesign (Integrity), a delivery saved by the squad (Teamwork).

Ask them back

“How does this squad balance speed of delivery against core-banking change-control and Shariah-governance cycles, working across Bengaluru and KL?” — shows you understand the real tension in this role.
