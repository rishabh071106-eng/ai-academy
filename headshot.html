Aim for the classic headshot proportions: eyes on the upper third line, a sliver of space above the hair, and the crop ending mid-chest. Faces read tiny in an org chart — fill the frame.
LinkedIn stores profile photos at up to 800×800 and crops them to a circle, so keep your chin and the top of your head inside the circular guide. Most org-chart tools render at 96px or smaller — export the 400px version and let them downscale.

Generating one with an AI model

If you'd rather have a model paint the headshot, feed it one of your own sharp, front-facing photos as the image reference — text alone will not preserve your likeness.

1. Image-to-image (best likeness)

Using the attached photo as the identity reference, produce a professional corporate
headshot of the same person. Keep the face, bone structure, hairline, eyebrows and
beard shape exactly as they are in the reference — do not slim, smooth or restyle
the face.

Framing: head-and-shoulders portrait, square 1:1 crop, eyes on the upper third,
small margin above the hair, crop ending mid-chest. Subject facing camera, shoulders
angled ~15 degrees, relaxed closed-mouth smile, direct eye contact with the lens.

Wardrobe: crisp white or light blue shirt with a well-fitted charcoal blazer,
collar sitting flat, no logos.

Lighting: soft key light from 45 degrees with a large softbox, subtle fill on the
shadow side, gentle hair light for separation. Natural skin texture retained —
visible pores, no plastic retouching.

Background: smooth neutral studio grey with a soft falloff, shallow depth of field.

Camera look: 85mm lens at f/2.8, eye-level, sharp on the near eye.
Output: photorealistic, colour, high resolution.

3. Negative prompt / things to rule out

Avoid: heavy skin smoothing, plastic or waxy skin, altered face shape, changed eye
colour, extra fingers, warped ears, crooked collar, harsh direct flash, blown-out
highlights, busy background, watermarks, text, hats, sunglasses, exaggerated jawline,
cartoon or 3D-render styling, oversaturated colour.

What separates a headshot from a selfie

  • Eye line high. Eyes sit about a third of the way down the frame. Centring your whole head leaves dead space above it.
  • Camera at eye level. Shooting from below is the single biggest giveaway. Raise the phone, or crop to hide the angle.
  • Light on the face, not behind it. Face a window. Backlight turns you into a silhouette.
  • Fill the circle. LinkedIn and every org chart crop to a circle at small sizes — a wide shot becomes an unrecognisable dot.
  • Plain background. Anything readable behind you competes with your face. Blur it or move.
  • Wear what you wear to work. The photo should match the person who walks into the meeting.
  • One photo everywhere. Same headshot on LinkedIn, Slack, email and the org chart — that repetition is how people learn your face.
