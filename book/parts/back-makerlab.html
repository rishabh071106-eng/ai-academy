The Back of the Book

The Maker Lab

Twelve hands-on projects to turn what you learned into things you build.

The very best way to truly learn AI is to make things — so here are twelve projects, sorted from easy paper games to ambitious builds. For anything that uses a computer or goes online, team up with a trusted adult first.

🔧 Project 1 — The 20-Questions Animal Sorter

Goal: build a decision tree on paper that can guess any animal.

You'll need: paper, a pencil, and a friend.

  1. Write a yes-or-no question at the top, like “Does it live in water?”
  2. Draw two branches — yes and no — and add a sharper question on each.
  3. Keep branching until each end leaf names one animal.
  4. Test it: have a friend pick an animal and follow your tree.

Connects to: Class 4 — Decision Trees.

🧪 Project 2 — Build Your Own Dataset

Goal: collect real examples and describe them with features.

You'll need: a handful of buttons or leaves, paper, a ruler.

  1. Gather 10–15 buttons (or leaves) and lay them in a row.
  2. Make a table with columns: color, size, shape, holes (or veins).
  3. Fill in one row per object — that table is your dataset.

Connects to: Class 2 — Data & Features.

🧩 Project 3 — Nearest-Neighbor Classmates

Goal: predict an answer by looking at the closest neighbors.

You'll need: graph paper and a few classmates.

  1. Plot each classmate as a dot using two facts, like height and shoe size.
  2. Color each dot by a label, such as “likes soccer” or “likes art.”
  3. Add a brand-new dot. Find its 3 nearest dots and let them vote on its label.

Connects to: Class 5 — Nearest Neighbors.

🎨 Project 4 — Sort the Mystery Bag

Goal: discover groups with no labels at all — clustering.

You'll need: a mixed bag of small objects (coins, beads, pasta).

  1. Pour them out and stare for a moment — don't decide groups yet.
  2. Push similar objects together until natural clumps appear.
  3. Now name each clump. You found the groups the data already had.

Connects to: Class 6 — Clustering.

📊 Project 5 — The Plant Predictor

Goal: draw a line of best fit and predict the future.

You'll need: a growing plant, a ruler, graph paper.

  1. Each week, measure your plant's height and plot a dot (week vs. height).
  2. After 4–5 weeks, draw one straight line that passes near all the dots.
  3. Extend the line to next week and read off your prediction. Then check it!

Connects to: Class 7 — Regression & Prediction.

🤖 Project 6 — Train a Browser Model

Goal: teach a real model in the browser to tell two hand signs apart.

You'll need: a computer with a webcam — and a trusted adult.

  1. With your adult, open a teachable-machine-style training tool.
  2. Make two classes, like “thumbs up” and “open hand,” and show many examples of each.
  3. Press Train, then test it live. Add more examples where it gets confused.

Connects to: Class 9 — Training a Model.

🔍 Project 7 — How Computers See

Goal: decode a picture made only of numbers, pixel by pixel.

You'll need: graph paper and a pencil.

  1. Draw a 10×10 grid. Fill some squares dark, leave others blank.
  2. Write 1 in every dark square and 0 in every blank one.
  3. Hand the grid of 0s and 1s to a friend — can they redraw your picture?

Connects to: Class 10 — Computer Vision.

🃏 Project 8 — Be a Language Model

Goal: predict the next word, just like a chatbot does.

You'll need: two or more players and your imagination.

  1. One player says a starting word, like “The.”
  2. Each player adds the single most likely next word, in turn.
  3. Read the sentence aloud. Notice how guessing one word at a time builds a story.

Connects to: Class 11 — Language Models.

🎵 Project 9 — Clicker Training a Friend

Goal: shape a behavior with rewards — reinforcement learning, live.

You'll need: a willing friend and small rewards (claps or stickers).

  1. Secretly pick an action for them to do, like “touch the lamp.”
  2. Say nothing about the goal. Reward them only when they get warmer.
  3. Watch them learn the secret action from rewards alone.

Connects to: Class 12 — Reinforcement Learning.

🗺️ Project 10 — Maze Search by Numbers

Goal: find the shortest path the way a search algorithm does.

You'll need: graph paper and a pencil.

  1. Draw a grid maze with walls, a start cell, and a goal cell.
  2. Write 0 on the start. Write 1 on every neighbor, then 2, then 3, spreading outward.
  3. From the goal, step back down the numbers (…3, 2, 1, 0) to trace the shortest path.

Connects to: Class 13 — Search & Pathfinding.

🏆 Project 11 — The Taste-Twin Survey

Goal: recommend something by matching people with similar tastes.

You'll need: a short survey and a few friends.

  1. Ask everyone to rate 5 movies (or snacks) from 1 to 5.
  2. For you, find the friend whose ratings are closest — your “taste twin.”
  3. Recommend yourself something your twin loved that you haven't tried.

Connects to: Class 14 — Recommendation Systems.

💡 Project 12 — The Fairness Audit

Goal: inspect an everyday app for fairness, then write your maker pledge.

You'll need: a notebook, and an adult to explore the app with.

  1. Pick an app that suggests things (videos, music, games).
  2. Ask: who might it treat unfairly? What does it never show you? Write down 3 worries.
  3. Write your own AI Maker Pledge — the promises you'll keep when you build.

Connects to: Class 15 — Fairness & AI Ethics.

You started this book curious, and now you can build. Pick one project, make it real, then share it with someone — every maker you admire began exactly here.

🏅 The AI Maker Pledge

  • I will test my work and fix it when it's wrong.
  • I will build AI that is fair and safe for everyone.
  • I will keep humans in charge of the important choices.
