The Back of the Book

For Grown-Ups & Teachers

A few honest words for the parents, teachers, and librarians who get to share these stories with a curious young mind.

Thank you for opening this book with a child. Whatever you call yourself — parent, grandparent, teacher, mentor, the grown-up who answers the hard questions at bedtime — you are the most important part of how a young reader will come to understand artificial intelligence. This note is for you.

What this book is — and isn't

This is an intuition-builder, not a math textbook. Its job is to give a child a sturdy, accurate mental picture of how AI actually works, so that nothing later in their life — no headline, no chatbot, no classroom — feels like pure magic. Every story at Whisker Woods Academy dramatizes a real algorithm or idea: nearest neighbours, decision trees, gradient descent, neural networks, transformers, reinforcement learning, and more.

To make those ideas reachable for a ten-year-old, we simplify — but we try to simplify honestly. We leave out the calculus, not the truth. Where a story rounds a sharp corner, the Word Bank hands the child the proper grown-up vocabulary, the same terms a working scientist would use, so the simplification becomes a doorway rather than a wall. Every Word Bank term is collected again in The AI Dictionary at the back.

How the book is organized

There are 21 classes arranged in five parts that build like blocks in a tower:

  • Part 1 — What AI Even Is: the big picture, and how machines can learn from examples instead of rules.
  • Part 2 — Classic Machine Learning: the dependable algorithms that quietly run much of the world.
  • Part 3 — Neural Networks & Deep Learning: how layers of tiny decisions add up to something that can see and hear.
  • Part 4 — Language, Agents & Decisions: how machines work with words, take actions, and learn by trying.
  • Part 5 — The Big Ideas: generative AI, fairness and bias, safety, and a maker finale where everything comes together.

At the very back you'll also find The AI Dictionary, a kid-friendly glossary, and The Maker Lab, a pack of projects for readers who want to build as well as understand.

🧭 How to use it

Read it aloud together, or let a confident reader explore on their own. Take one class at a time — there's no prize for rushing. Above all, do the Try It Yourself activities: doing beats reading, every time, and these turn an abstract idea into something the hands remember. When a class clicks, pair it with a matching project from The Maker Lab.

Most activities are deliberately unplugged — paper, pencils, and things around the house. Anything that involves a computer or the internet should be done with a trusted adult, which is a lovely excuse to sit down and learn alongside your reader.

A word on AI safety for families

We want children to leave this book both curious and critical — delighted by what AI can do, and clear-eyed about what it can't. A few truths are worth saying plainly, together, more than once.

⚠️ What to talk about

AI can be confidently wrong. A chatbot may state a made-up fact — a hallucination — in the same calm voice it uses for true ones. AI can also be biased, because it learns from human data that carries human unfairness. And AI should never replace a child's own judgment or kindness. It is a tool for thinking with, not a authority to obey. Make a habit of talking together about what your reader makes, sees, and is told online.

💬 Talk About It

A handful of questions to ask over dinner, in the car, or after a class:

  • What's something a computer is better at than a person — and something a person will always be better at?
  • If an AI made a decision about you, how would you want it to be fair?
  • When should you trust what an AI tells you, and when should you check it yourself?
  • Can you spot a place AI is already part of your day — and did you notice it before?
  • If you could build any AI to help people, what would it do?
  • What's a job you'd never want a machine to do, and why?
💡 The one thing to remember

A child who understands how AI works is a child who can't be fooled by it — and who might one day make it kinder. That understanding starts with curiosity, and you are holding the spark.

So: turn the page, meet the animals, and let the questions fly. We hope these stories give your young reader a lifetime of clear thinking and brave wondering — and we hope you have as much fun reading them aloud as we had writing them.
